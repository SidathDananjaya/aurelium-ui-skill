# Marketing and landing pages

A landing page has one job: move the visitor to one action. Everything that
does not serve that action is a candidate for deletion.

## User goals

Visitors arrive sceptical and in a hurry. They want to know what this is,
whether it is for them, whether it works, and what it costs, in that order.

## Primary tasks

| Task | Where it is served |
|---|---|
| Understand what this is | The first viewport, in under five seconds |
| Decide whether it is for them | Proof and use cases |
| Judge whether it works | Evidence, not adjectives |
| Understand the cost | Pricing, without a maze |
| Act | One primary action, repeated |

## Information architecture

1. **Hero.** What it is, who for, one action.
2. **Proof.** Logos, numbers, a quote with a real name and role.
3. **How it works.** Three steps at most.
4. **Features as outcomes.** What the user can now do.
5. **Deeper proof.** A case study or measurable result.
6. **Pricing.** Plain, with the common plan marked.
7. **Objection handling.** The questions sales actually gets.
8. **Final call to action.**
9. **Footer.** Navigation, legal, contact.

Not every page needs all nine. Every page needs one, two, and the final action.

## Key screens

- Landing page.
- Pricing, if it does not fit inline.
- A post-conversion page, which is the moment most often wasted.

## The first viewport

The headline states the outcome, not the category. "Book direct and keep the
difference" beats "A platform for hospitality businesses".

- One headline, one supporting sentence, one primary action.
- The supporting sentence says who it is for.
- No carousel. No auto-playing video with sound.
- If there is a hero image, it earns its place by showing the product or the
  real thing, not a stock photograph.

**Check.** Cover everything below the fold. Can a stranger say what this is and
who it is for? If not, the hero has failed.

## Critical states

| State | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Form submitted | Confirmation naming what happens next and when |
| Form error | Preserves every entered value, names the fix |
| Pricing unavailable | Never show a broken price. Show a contact route |
| Slow connection | Hero text renders before the hero image |
| JavaScript disabled | The page still reads and the form still submits |

## Luxury moments

**The first viewport.** The only part everyone sees. It deserves
disproportionate effort: the typography, the spacing, the single clear action.

**The final call to action.** By this point the visitor is convinced or gone.
For the convinced, make acting trivial. Restate the outcome, not the feature.

**The post-conversion page.** Almost universally neglected. A thank-you page
that says what happens next, when, and how to prepare is the cheapest trust you
will ever buy.

## Pricing

- Three plans at most. Hick's law applies hardest here.
- Mark the common choice, honestly.
- State what happens at the limits of each plan.
- Show the annual and monthly price, with the saving stated in currency.
- Never hide the price behind a demo request unless the product genuinely has
  no list price. Say so plainly if it does not.

## Copy

Every claim is specific and checkable. "Faster" means nothing. "Checkout in
three taps" means something.

Use the customer's words, not internal vocabulary. See
`../foundations/microcopy.md`.

No exclamation marks. No countdown timers manufacturing urgency that is not
real: it reads as cheap, which is the opposite of the goal.

## Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it fails | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Three-card feature grid by default | The laziest layout in the genre | Structure the page around the argument |
| Carousel hero | Users rarely see slide two | One message |
| Competing calls to action | Splits attention, converts neither | One primary, repeated |
| Stock photography of people at laptops | Reads as filler on sight | Real product, or nothing |
| Vague claims | Unfalsifiable and unconvincing | Specific, checkable numbers |
| Testimonials with no name or role | Read as fabricated | Real name, role, company |
| Pricing behind a demo request | Loses everyone not ready to talk | Publish the price |
| Countdown timers on evergreen offers | Transparently false urgency | Remove |
| Cookie banner covering the hero | The first impression is an obstacle | Compact, non-blocking |
| Animating everything on scroll | Slows reading, fights the content | Motion for emphasis, once or twice |

## Recommended directions and dials

| Sector | Direction | Opulence | Density | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hospitality, fashion, property | Quiet Luxury | 6 | 2 | 4 |
| Premium B2B, finance | Obsidian | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Consumer, lifestyle, food | Tactile Craft | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| Developer tools, agencies | Swiss Precision | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| AI, infrastructure, security | Functional Futurism | 5 | 5 | 4 |

Landing pages run lower density and slightly higher opulence than the product
they advertise. This is the one place where a signature detail earns its cost.
